As of 2026-10-03. Research package: [`research_slowdown_2023_2025/`](../../research_slowdown_2023_2025/README.md).

## Completed

- Compared FY2023–FY2025 reported trajectories for Chanel, Hermès, LVMH Fashion & Leather Goods, and Gucci. The result depends on the year, metric, and reporting object; it does not support a universal Chanel resilience ranking.
- Located Chanel's recovery by region and by retail/wholesale channel. In reported USD, Europe and the Americas offset Asia Pacific's continued decline in FY2025. Retail remained below its FY2023 level.
- Reconciled Chanel's filed consolidated gross-profit, functional-expense, and operating-profit bridge. FY2025 revenue stabilization coexisted with a large operating-profit gap against FY2023.
- Aligned selected campaign and product events to when merchandise was available. Blazy's SS2026 products arrived in March 2026; they do not explain FY2025 product sales. Early ownership reports qualify any claim that CHANEL 25 first became available globally in March 2025.
- Collected selected public purchase/use accounts and third-party beauty scale evidence to define mechanisms. These do not establish representative customer behavior, category contribution, or marketing effectiveness.
- Produced the integrated Chinese argument and read-only reconciliation inputs. No handbag-only sales or causal marketing estimate was available.

## Current conclusion

Chanel looked different in FY2025 because group revenue stabilized while some peers continued to contract. Geography and channel data show where reported revenue changed. They do not identify the customer, category, price/volume, or marketing cause. Chanel's operating profit remained materially below FY2023, so the conclusion is not broad demand or profit immunity.
